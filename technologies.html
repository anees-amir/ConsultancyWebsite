Technologies

The building blocks of privacy-preserving AI.

A short guide to the technologies we use to design federated learning systems.

01

ML models

FL is model-agnostic. The right choice depends on your data and task.

Logistic regression

Lightweight and ideal for tabular data on resource-constrained participants.

Neural networks

Learn complex patterns in structured data; the backbone of most FL work.

Convolutional neural networks

Image and signal data, e.g. medical imaging across hospitals.

Decision trees

Interpretable models; gradient-boosted variants like XGBoost federate well.

02

Data partitioning

How data is split across participants determines the FL setup.

Horizontal

Same features, different records, e.g. hospitals sharing a patient schema.

Vertical

Same individuals, different features, e.g. a bank and a retailer.

Combined

Differs in both. The most complex case, common in cross-organisation work.

03

Privacy techniques

Extra protection so sensitive information isn't exposed, even to the server.

Differential privacy

Calibrated noise on model updates limits what can be learned about any individual.

Data masking

Identifiers are redacted or pseudonymised before training.

Homomorphic encryption

The server aggregates encrypted updates without ever decrypting them.

04

Aggregation strategies

How local updates are combined into one global model each round.

FedAvg

Weighted average of client models, proportional to each client's data size.

FedProx

Adds a proximal term to keep training stable when client data differs widely.

05

FL frameworks

Open-source tools that handle communication and orchestration.

Flower

Framework-agnostic (PyTorch, TensorFlow, JAX, scikit-learn) and production-ready.

TensorFlow Federated

Google's TensorFlow-native framework, strong for research and simulation.
